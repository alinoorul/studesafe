Checkpoint-based student safety

Track the handoff.
Not the child.

Every school run already has an adult confirming it — a driver, a gate guard, a teacher. StudeSafe captures that confirmation at every handoff and tells parents instantly, so a missed checkpoint becomes an alert within minutes, not a mystery hours later.

Request a demo See how it works

The core idea

No device on the child. No blind spots in the commute.

Don't put a location device on the child — track the handoffs instead: home, vehicle, school, vehicle, home. A QR code on the student's ID answers "who is this" when someone scans it, nothing more. It doesn't broadcast location on its own, and there's nothing for a child to lose, remove, or forget to carry. The vehicle carries the GPS. The child just carries an ID badge.

How it works

One route, five confirmations

  1. 01

    Home

    The day starts with a scanned confirmation at pickup — the first link in the chain.

  2. 02

    Vehicle

    The driver scans the student's badge on boarding and again on alighting — bus door or carpool car, same flow either way.

  3. 03

    School gate

    A guard or teacher scans on arrival, or the school's existing gate and biometric system reports the same event automatically.

  4. 04

    Classroom

    Where a school already runs digital attendance, that becomes a checkpoint too — no new hardware forced into the room.

  5. 05

    Return

    The same chain runs in reverse for the ride home, ending with a "reached home stop" notification.

What's included

Everything a checkpoint system needs, nothing it doesn't

QR ID card

A simple QR code on the student's badge. It answers "who is this" when scanned, nothing more — no GPS, no battery, nothing to track a child who isn't carrying it.

Checkpoint scanning

Drivers, gate staff, and teachers confirm a handoff in seconds, on a phone they already carry.

Live vehicle tracking

GPS stays on the bus or car, not the student, so parents can watch the actual commute and see an ETA in real time.

Notifications & escalation

A message lands the moment a checkpoint happens. If one doesn't happen on time, the system escalates — parent, then transport coordinator, then school admin — before it becomes a panic.

Carpool mode

No reader to install in a private car. The driver scans each rider's badge from their own phone, in and out — same trail, same alerts.

Approach alerts

A geofence-based nudge when the bus is close to the drop point, so someone's actually at the gate when it arrives.

Why this approach

Why we didn't just put a tracker on the child

  • A tracker is something to lose, remove, or forget. A checkpoint isn't.
  • Nothing broadcasts a child's location on its own — the QR only answers "who is this" when someone scans it.
  • It works inside the rules a school already has, without handing a phone to every student.
  • The failure that actually matters — a child who never should have left one point but did — becomes an alert within minutes, not a last-known-location from three hours ago.

Safety & privacy

Built around one rule: track the handoff, not the person

The QR code carries no personal information — only an identifier that means nothing outside the system. Every account sees only what its role needs: a driver sees their route, a teacher sees their class, a parent sees their own child. We're building this with student data protection in mind from the ground up, including how long data is kept and who can access it.

FAQ

Questions worth answering upfront

Does this track my child directly?

No. StudeSafe tracks handoffs, not the child. The only thing that moves is the vehicle's GPS — the student carries a QR badge that simply confirms identity when scanned.

What if our school doesn't have scanners yet?

It works from day one with manual QR scanning by staff on the phones they already carry. It can connect to a school's existing gate or biometric attendance system later, so nothing has to be replaced to get started.

What happens if a checkpoint is missed?

If an expected checkpoint doesn't happen inside its time window, the system escalates automatically — starting with the parent, then the transport coordinator, then school admin — so a human is looking into it while there's still time to just check.
